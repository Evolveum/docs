= Simulation
:page-toc: top
:page-since: "4.7"
:page-description: This article introduces the midPoint simulation feature and explains how it removes the "fear factor" from deploying new configurations.
:page-keywords: simulation, test, testing, preview, development environment, task, activity, preview mode, development configuration, simulation result

Simulation in midPoint is the feature that removes the "fear factor" from deploying new configurations, connecting new resources, or adjusting the production environment in general.

MidPoint is an identity governance and administration system and as such, it is designed to play the mission-critical role in the ecosystem of your organization safely, helping you avoid stressful situations as much as possible.
This article describes how midPoint lets you _look before you leap_.

An identity governance deployment is never finished.
Roles get redesigned, mappings get corrected, new resources get onboarded, and cleanup campaigns run for months after go-live.
Every one of those changes touches the real production accounts, so deploying it without prior testing is, in essence, a leap of faith:
you believe the configuration is right, and if it is not, you find out the hard way — often at night or during the peak hours when everyone depends on you fixing it fast.

Simulation removes that leap of faith.

== What is simulation

Simulation lets a task compute exactly what a change would do, based on the actual live data, and record the outcome — but apply nothing.
Nothing is written to the midPoint repository or any connected resource.
The system state remains untouched until you decide otherwise.

The crucial property is that a simulated task yields the same results as if it were not a simulation: it simply reports what the outcome would be instead of carrying it out.

Before simulation existed in midPoint, the only way to preview a change was to maintain a separate test instance and preview the change there first.
That approach is expensive to build, painful to maintain, and it has a dangerous failure mode: as soon as the copy drifts from the real data — and copies always drift — the preview stops being representative.
A change that looks harmless on stale test data can have serious consequences on live data.
For this reason, when you had no way to simulate on real data, it was necessary to deploy changes at night or on weekends to avoid business disruption in case anything went awry.

All those pains go away when you can simulate using real current data.
Because simulation in midPoint runs on the production system with production data, its answers are about the world in which you actually operate, not a replica of it from last week.
That closes the gap between "it worked in the test environment" and "it works here".

Curious how it works? Read xref:/midpoint/reference/simulation/[].

== What you get with simulation

* *Confidence before you commit:* you see exactly what a change would do to real objects, before it does anything.
* *Fast feedback:* spot something wrong, adjust the proposed configuration, rerun, and get updated results in minutes rather than days.
* *Evidence for decision-making:* results come as structured figures — how many objects are affected, what changes would be made, where processing would fail — which is exactly what stakeholders and auditors want to see.
* *No test-environment maintenance:* the sandbox approach, with its cost and its silent drift, stops being necessary.

== Typical use cases

Typical situations where simulation pays for itself include:

* *Initial onboarding and cleanup:* early rounds of importing data from new resources, where you are still discovering what the source systems actually contain.
* *Validating configuration changes:* any adjustment to roles, mappings, or resources that must be proven safe before it is allowed to touch real data.
* *Rollouts of new or changed policies:* showing with numbers rather than assurances what effect a policy change would have on the population.
